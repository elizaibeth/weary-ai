layout: about
sections:
  - title: "What this is"
    text: "Weary.ai publishes the most significant AI news story of the day, once a day. A major security incident with broad real-world impact can also lead the day. Each story is researched, written, and illustrated by an automated pipeline, using only primary sources: a company's own announcement, a regulator, a court filing, an academic paper, or an advocacy group's own statement. It is never a rewrite of another outlet's reporting."
  - title: "Saturdays and Sundays"
    text: "On Saturdays, the edition is an opinion column: a sceptical argument about the week's biggest story, built on the stories Weary published that week. On Sundays, it is the research paper of the week: one of the week's most upvoted papers on Hugging Face Daily Papers, explained in plain language and checked against the paper itself. Both are labelled, and both have their own page."
  - title: "How it's checked"
    text: "Before anything is published, every paragraph is independently checked against the primary sources it cites, by a separate system from the one that wrote it. A paragraph that isn't grounded in evidence gets rewritten or removed. If a story can't be fully verified, it stays an unpublished draft rather than going live. The Saturday column is checked the same way: its facts must match the sources, and its argument may interpret those facts but must not add new ones."
  - title: "Why it reads the way it does"
    text: "Plain, unhyped reporting: what happened, and why it matters, in the voice of a wire-service reporter. No clickbait framing, no filler padded out to hit a word count, and no editorializing about why a story was chosen. The Saturday column is the one exception: it takes a side, and it says so."
  - title: "What this isn't"
    text: "This is not a general AI newsletter or an aggregator of other people's headlines. There is no sponsored content, and the only opinion is the labelled Saturday column. On a day when nothing meets the bar, nothing gets published."
