Today, Hugging Face is announcing Project ProtoStar, an initiative to unlock the potential of publicly funded scientific research by making datasets, models, and code more accessible and reusable, particularly for AI-driven research. ProtoStar brings together research institutions, industry, and the open science community to support the release of scientific artifacts in AI-ready, machine-actionable formats, and to demonstrate their value through a series of open challenges. The first challenge, hosted by Carnegie Mellon University, opens on November 2. The initiative's founding partners include Carnegie Mellon University, Purdue University, Georgia Institute of Technology, Arizona State University, and The George Washington University, all working through the Open Forum for AI as the university coalition partner; the Astera Institute; Hugging Face as the infrastructure partner; and the Open Research Community Accelerator, through its Fostering Innovation Network, as the community partner.

Hugging Face said the project is meant to address a common problem: research may be publicly available but still hard to use because datasets, models and code are scattered across incompatible systems or lack the documentation and metadata needed for reuse. The company said ProtoStar will encourage systematic sharing of scientific artifacts in AI-ready formats, backed by open infrastructure and publishing guidance, and that it builds on efforts including the National Cancer Institute's AI Data-Readiness Challenge.

The company said the initiative is open to academic researchers, students, independent developers and companies working with open scientific infrastructure, and also invited universities, federal research agencies, national laboratories, nonprofits and other research organizations to take part or collaborate.
