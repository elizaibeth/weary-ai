Researchers behind a new paper propose a work-centric framework for agents that handle digital tasks across tools, applications and workflows, arguing that existing research does not offer a unified way to explain how such systems reliably complete delegated work. The paper, titled "Work Agents: Systems, Capabilities, and Optimization," treats work agents as systems that combine foundation models and runtime harnesses to operate in stateful workspaces and produce verifiable outcomes.

The authors divide work conditions along two dimensions, which they call work persistence and work openness, and sort digital work into five forms based on task scope and organization. They also identify five capability families they say are needed for reliable work execution, then examine model optimization, harness optimization and joint optimization as ways to improve performance.

The paper also reviews evaluation methods and metrics from the standpoint of work outcomes, and points to several directions for later study, including sustained delegation, adaptation to open environments and the relationship between work execution and capability improvement. The abstract frames the work as a framework for understanding, optimizing and evaluating work agents, but it does not present a system release or experimental result in the passages provided.
