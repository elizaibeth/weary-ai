Microsoft released a package of initiatives tied to its datacenters intended to boost local employment, expand training through the Datacenter Academy, and increase transparency around water use. It notes that LinkedIn data show job postings for data center occupations grew 23 percent globally and 13.5 percent in the US in 2025, underscoring a wider demand for skilled labor.

The company describes expanding its Datacenter Academy program in partnership with local colleges and vocational schools, with examples in Boydton, Virginia, where the campus has helped hundreds earn industry-recognized IT and critical facilities certifications. A 2024 expansion opened the Critical Environment Training Lab in South Hill, using decommissioned datacenter equipment for hands-on training.

As part of its transparency push, Microsoft said it will publish water-use data for each datacenter region and report on replenishment efforts, framing water management as deposits and withdrawals in a regional “bank account” approach to local ecosystems.

The post also describes advocacy for public policies to minimize water use and strengthen resilience, including promoting reclaimed and industrial recycled water as the default supply for datacenters where feasible and aiming to harmonize transparency standards.

In Quincy, Washington, the company cites a long-running presence that it says has supported thousands of construction jobs and created permanent roles tied to its datacenters; it notes a drop in the share of residents below the poverty line from 29.4 percent in 2013 to 13.1 percent in 2023, and a tripling of county tax revenues over two decades.

The company says it will pursue a civically responsible approach in all regions where it builds, owns, and operates datacenters to support public services, healthcare, schools, libraries, and parks.
