Anthropic published three public measurements to illuminate the pace of frontier AI development inside labs. They measure the share of AI R&D performed by AI, how closely autonomous agents are overseen, and how compute is allocated to safety work. In the accompanying snapshot, Claude accounts for 26 percent of Anthropic's AI R&D, and more than 90 percent of AI R&D is at or above the AI collaboration level on a five level automation scale from AL0 to AL5. As of August 2026, Claude is not operating autonomously for any measured subset of AI R&D work. The snapshot also reports about 30,000 agents active on Anthropic's primary internal platform, with online monitors blocking roughly 0.002 percent of agent actions. Compute figures show six percent of total AI R&D compute, and twelve percent of AI driven AI R&D compute specifically, allocated to safety work.

As of August 2026, there were about 30,000 agents doing research and engineering work on Anthropic's primary internal platform. Online monitors check actions in real time and either block dangerous actions or steer misbehavior automatically, while offline monitors read agent activities after the fact. In the reported figures, about 0.002% of agent actions were blocked by online monitors.

On compute, the report says about 6% of total AI R&D compute is devoted to safety work, and about 12% of AI-driven AI R&D compute specifically goes to safety-related activities.

Anthropic also says it plans to embed independent third-party evaluators from multiple organizations to review safety practices, report incidents, and assess model-training pipelines and processes.

In a separate essay, Dario Amodei argues for pacing frontier AI development, outlining a three-step plan that starts with embedded evaluators, followed by democratic coordination and global coordination to strengthen safety while preserving commercial progress.

CASRAI’s coverage notes the release occurred on September 17, 2026 and adds that no outside party has independently verified these numbers.

Analysts describe the measures as part of a broader discussion about how quickly frontier AI should advance, a debate that Amodei frames as balancing safety and capability gains.
