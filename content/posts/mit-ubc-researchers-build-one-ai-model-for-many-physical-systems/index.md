Researchers at MIT, the University of British Columbia and other institutions said they have built a single deep-learning system, called WorldParticle, that can model different physical processes with one architecture. The system is designed to handle cases from sliding sand to folding proteins, which the researchers said are usually covered by separate simulators built for specific equations and material behavior.

The model treats each material as particles and predicts how those particles move step by step. The researchers said that approach lets the system adapt to materials, forces and environments it did not see during training, and could be used for problems such as fluid sloshing against a deformable solid or granular material under a soft robot.

The researchers said the longer-term goal is to move toward a general-purpose simulation pipeline for scientific discovery. Guo said a unified model could help scientists discover or explain physical phenomena, though the statement did not give a timeline for that work.
