Anthropic closed an IPO of 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83, with about $91.6 billion received by Anthropic and no proceeds from shares sold by selling stockholders. This is the part meant to look simple. A public debut. A large capital raise. But the telling detail is that the same deal also gave holders who already had shares a way to sell without that money going to the company.

Certain selling stockholders sold 23,648,646 Class A shares, and Anthropic said it received no proceeds from those sales. That is not a scandal. It is a reminder. The banner says new public company; one quiet function is liquidity for people already holding the stock.

Anthropic also sold 313,253,007 Class N shares to Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google, Micron Technology, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and TSMC Partners in a private placement for about $26.0 billion. So the public door was not the only door. The more revealing door was reserved for companies able to write the large cheque. Public offering, private room.

Aggregate gross proceeds from the IPO, including selling stockholder shares, and the concurrent private placement were about $119.5 billion before fees and expenses.

The underwriters fully exercised their option to buy an additional 147,000,000 Class A shares from Anthropic at the IPO price. That does not make it less revealing. It still looks designed to satisfy more than one purpose, with public trading only part of the story.

The private placement shares were Class N common stock and were sold at the same $83 public offering price. That is the tell. The deal looks less like a plain public debut and more like something shaped by special access. The real point is the structure, not the headline.
