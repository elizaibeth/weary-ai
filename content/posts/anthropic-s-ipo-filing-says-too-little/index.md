Anthropic said it filed an S-1 registration statement for a proposed IPO of Class A common stock. It said the number of shares and price range have not been determined. That is the problem. The filing announcement supplies the ritual, not the investment case. A race without terms is not a race investors can judge.

Anthropic said the proposed offering will be made only by prospectus, with copies of the preliminary prospectus available when available from Goldman Sachs or Morgan Stanley. So the useful paper is not this notice. It is just a placeholder.

Anthropic said about 500 US federal, state and local government organizations use Claude. It also said a GSA agreement gives government partners a 40% discount, and agencies are not bound by a minimum spend commitment. This is the kind of growth story that flatters an IPO narrative. It should not be mistaken for proof of pricing power. Discounted public-sector adoption is still adoption, but the discount is the telling detail.

Anthropic said Utah gave Claude Code to 34 developers across 10 state agencies, and 71% reported saving more than 20 hours by the end of a four-week pilot. Useful. Small. Not enough to carry public-market excitement by itself. A pilot says a tool helped some users in a trial; it does not answer what the offering is worth.

Google dated its Gemini 4 Carbon announcement Oct. 28, 2026, and said access starts this week for a subset of cyber defenders before wider availability. Google also reported Carbon scores of 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8% on ExploitGym, and 71.0% on CWE-bench. Controlled access. Benchmark numbers. Big claims wrapped around limited public evidence. The seller benefits when lab scores are treated as proof of commercial certainty.

Anthropic said the share count and price range for its proposed offering have not been determined. So no, the filing does not justify the fresh IPO buzz. Investors are not short of slogans. They are short of terms. The filing says too little.
