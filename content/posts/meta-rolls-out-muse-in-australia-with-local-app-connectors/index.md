Meta said Muse is now available in Australia, with connector features rolling out progressively across creativity, shopping, property, fitness and entertainment. The company said users can connect Muse to services they already use, including Gmail, Google Calendar, Instagram and OpenTable, and named early local partners such as Canva, Domain, Kogan, MECCA, Officeworks, realestate.com.au, SWEAT, ShopBack and Ticketek. Meta said people remain in control of what Muse can access, and that sensitive actions such as sending an email or making a purchase require approval.

Meta said Muse runs on a dedicated cloud environment called Muse Secure VM, which stores data and credentials for connected services and keeps passwords and payment methods out of the agent’s view. In the company’s earlier announcement, Meta described Muse as a personal AI agent that can act on tasks after a person gives instructions, and said it is powered by the company’s Muse Spark model.

Meta first introduced Muse in September and said at the time that the agent could keep working after people close the app, return when something changes and check back before actions that need approval. In that earlier post, Meta also said Muse could use Link built by Stripe for checkout and that Shop Pay support and 1Password support were coming later.
