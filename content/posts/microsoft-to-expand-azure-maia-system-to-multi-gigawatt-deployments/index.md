Microsoft describes Maia 300 as using a software-defined dataflow architecture and AI-assisted kernel optimization to improve locality and reduce data movement, a combination the company says yields better performance per watt than traditional GPU designs.

Maia 200 is already in production, serving Microsoft Copilot and other workloads, and is supporting the MAI team as it develops next-generation models, with GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry slated to follow later this year.

Microsoft plans to scale the Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027, with Maia 200 already running production workloads and Maia 300 positioned for new AI tasks.

Maia 300 is described as a complete intelligent system designed for long-context reasoning, latency-sensitive conversations and sustained, multi-step tasks; the company says it should deliver about 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems, and, in a full 96-accelerator configuration, up to seven times the rack-scale throughput of Maia 200.

Beyond the hardware, Microsoft frames the Azure Maia program as foundational to its AI infrastructure strategy, emphasizing a multigenerational approach and close co-design between hardware and software.
